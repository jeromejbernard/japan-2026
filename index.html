Tokyo
Chapter One — The City That Never Stops
4 nights
17 – 21 December
▾
🏨
9h Capsule Hotel Akasaka
~$60pp/night · Gender-separated floors (men's floor / women's floor — no mixing) · Futuristic pods, spotlessly clean, slap bang in central Tokyo
Day 1Thu 17 DecTouchdown Tokyo
  • Land at Narita 6:15pm — Narita Express (N'EX) to Shinjuku, ~45 min, ~$37 AUD
  • Check in to 9h Capsule Hotel, Akasaka. Your pod awaits.
  • First konbini run — 7-Eleven Japan is a completely different beast. Onigiri, Asahi, Pocky.
  • Walk to Akasaka-Mitsuke. Get your first ramen. You'll immediately understand why you're here.
Days 2–418–20 DecTokyo — The Hit List
This is a non-exhaustive list — there's far more to Tokyo than three days can hold. Order TBC once we know everyone's priorities.
Sara's must-dos
  • 🎢 Tokyo Disneyland — book tickets well in advance, December fills fast. Full day.
  • 🌊 Tokyo DisneySea — a completely separate park, easily another full day on its own
  • 🎮 Pokémon Centre — multiple locations (Shibuya, Ikebukuro), free entry, dangerous for the wallet
  • 🏎️ Mario Kart through Tokyo — street go-karts in costume through Akihabara (Akiba Kart). Book ahead.
  • 👘 Harajuku — Takeshita Street for cosplay chaos, Omotesando for the upscale version
  • 🕹️ Akihabara — electronics towers, retro arcades, full sensory overload
  • ⛩️ Asakusa + Senso-ji — Nakamise Market for souvenirs, best before 8am when it's still quiet
  • 🍻 Shinjuku nights — Golden Gai (200+ tiny bars, each seats ~8), Omoide Yokocho for yakitori under the tracks
Also worth considering
  • 🐟 Tsukiji Outer Market — early morning breakfast sushi, freshest fish in the world
  • 🎨 TeamLab Planets — immersive digital art unlike anything in Australia. Book in advance.
  • 🗼 Tokyo Skytree (634m) — sunset views over the city, Shibuya Crossing nearby for the famous pedestrian scramble
  • 🏯 Meiji Shrine — forested walk in the middle of the city, a genuine breath of fresh air
Day 5 AMMon 21 DecShinkansen Morning
  • Check out of 9h Akasaka, luggage to lockers at Tokyo Station
  • Board the Nozomi at Tokyo Station — 300km/h, 2h 21min to Shin-Osaka
  • Sit on the right side (window seat D/E) for a Mt Fuji flyby

Central Osaka budget hotel — Namba or Shinsaibashi area

Day 5 PMMon 21 DecWelcome to the Food Capital
  • Check in, drop bags, head straight to Dotonbori — Osaka's iconic canal-side shopping and dining strip
  • Find the Glico Running Man sign on the Dotonbori canal — the most photographed spot in Osaka. You'll know it when you see it.
  • Dinner: take your pick — okonomiyaki (savoury pancake), takoyaki (octopus balls), or go bold with fugu (blowfish — an Osaka speciality, book a licensed restaurant ahead of time)
Days 6–722–23 DecOsaka — The Hit List
Non-exhaustive — Osaka rewards wandering. Order TBC once we know everyone's priorities.
On the plan
  • 🦌 Nara Deer Park — 45 min from Osaka, over 1,000 free-roaming deer. Feed them deer crackers. Unmissable.
  • ⛩️ Kyoto day trip — 30 min by Shinkansen. Fushimi Inari torii gates, Arashiyama Bamboo Grove, Nishiki Market
Also worth considering
  • 🏯 Osaka Castle — feudal Japan on a moat, surrounded by a park perfect for a morning walk
  • 🐟 Kuromon Ichiba Market — 180 stalls, freshest seafood in the city, ideal for breakfast
  • 🏙️ Shinsekai — retro 1930s neighbourhood, local kushikatsu joints, zero tourists
  • 🌆 Umeda Sky Building — dramatic floating garden observatory, best visited at night
  • 🛍️ Shinsaibashi shopping arcade — covered mall stretching for blocks, great for fashion and souvenirs
  • 🎢 Universal Studios Japan — Nintendo World is the drawcard. Worth it if the group wants another theme park day.
Day 8 AMThu 24 DecBack to Tokyo — Christmas Eve!
  • Check out Osaka hotel, Nozomi Shinkansen back to Tokyo (2h 21min)
  • Check back into 9h Akasaka — same crew, same neighbourhood, same energy

Tokyo
Christmas in a City That Stays Open
3 nights
24 – 27 December
▾
🏨
9h Capsule Hotel Akasaka
~$60pp/night · Back to base · Christmas in Tokyo is nothing like Christmas in Australia — in the best possible way
Day 8 PMThu 24 DecChristmas Eve in Tokyo
  • Check in, drop bags — back at 9h Akasaka, same neighbourhood, same energy
  • KFC Christmas dinner — yes, it's a genuine Japanese tradition. Book the set meal in advance or grab walk-in buckets on the night.
  • Christmas illuminations: Roppongi Hills, Tokyo Midtown, Marunouchi — the entire city is dressed up. Nothing like Christmas in Brisbane.
  • Christmas Eve in Japan is a romantic couples' celebration — the streets are warm, busy, and magical
Days 9–1025–26 DecChristmas & Boxing Day — The Hit List
Non-exhaustive — and anything not covered in Tokyo Phase 1 rolls straight into here. Order TBC.
The big one — Christmas Day
  • Japan does not shut down for Christmas. Every restaurant, shop, and bar is open. This is the Christmas you've always deserved.
  • 🎨 TeamLab Borderless — newly reopened in Azabudai Hills, immersive digital art on a massive scale. Book ahead.
  • ♨️ Onsen day spa — soak the legs. After 8 days of walking, you've earned it.
  • 🍳 Kewpie Mayo Factory tour — free, fascinating, and very Japanese. Needs to be booked well in advance.
Also worth considering
  • 🛍️ Shibuya 109 + Isetan Shinjuku — final shopping assault before Hakuba. Isetan's basement food hall (depachika) is the finest curated food in Japan under one roof.
  • 🍜 Ichiran ramen — solo booths, rich tonkotsu broth. The perfect pre-Hakuba carb load.
  • 🏘️ Yanaka — old shitamachi neighbourhood, temples, cats, tiny galleries. Like stepping back 60 years.
  • 🌿 Shinjuku Gyoen — one of Tokyo's best gardens, peaceful and completely underrated in winter
  • ⛰️ Mount Takao day hike — 1 hour from Shinjuku, easy trail, great views of Mt Fuji on a clear day
  • 🐱 Cat café — exactly what it sounds like.
Day 11Sun 27 DecHakuba Transfer Day
  • Check out of 9h Akasaka, coach departs Shinjuku Station for Hakuba (~5 hours)
  • Arrive Echoland, Hakuba village, mid-afternoon
  • Check in to Monkey Rider Lodge — your home for 7 nights
  • Collect snowboard/ski gear from Central Snowsports Hakuba (4 stores in the village)
  • Dinner at Echoland. First mountain meal. Tomorrow, you shred.

The Hakuba Valley by the numbers

Hosted the alpine events at the 1998 Winter Olympics. 10 interconnected resorts, one valley liftpass. Japan's powder is legendary — light, dry, and deep. You will understand on Day 1.

Day 12Sun 28 DecFirst Tracks
  • Hakuba Valley Liftpass activates — valid at Happo-One, Hakuba47, Goryu, Tsugaike, Norikura, Cortina, Jiigatake, Kashimayari and Sanosaka
  • Start at Happo-One — Japan's most famous resort. 13 lifts. Big mountain energy.
  • Jerry as your on-mountain guide — knows the lines, knows the secret runs
  • Après-ski back at the Monkey Rider bar. The trip has truly begun.
Day 13Mon 29 DecHakuba 47 & Goryu
  • Hakuba47 + Goryu — interconnected resorts, 37 runs combined, terrain park for those who like sending it
  • Mountain ramen at the lodge mid-mountain. Non-negotiable.
  • Complimentary 2-hour guided orientation (Monkey Rider perk)
Day 14Tue 30 DecTsugaike Kogen
  • Tsugaike — massive resort, fewer crowds, incredible powder fields off-piste
  • Long groomed runs for those who want speed, backcountry for those who want powder
  • 🍔 Burger King at the gondola mid-station — yes, a full Burger King, famously out of place, right there on the mountain. Lunch sorted.
  • Mimizuku Onsen in the evening (5 min by car) — wood-fired bath, traditional experience
Day 15Wed 31 DecNew Year's Eve in the Alps
  • Morning session — one last lap before the party starts
  • NYE in Echoland — Hakuba's village hub goes absolutely off. Multiple bars and clubs, all packed, all buzzing from dusk until well into the new year
  • Hakuba has a massive, loyal Australian following — the NYE crowd is a brilliant mix of Aussie travellers, Japanese locals, and like-minded people from around the world who all made the same great decision you did
  • The energy is unlike anything back home — people who love mountains, love snow, love adventure, and love a good time. Your people.
  • Ringing in 2027 surrounded by the Japanese Alps, powder under your feet, your best friends beside you — this is what it's all about
  • There is no better place on earth to see in the New Year. Full stop.
Day 16Thu 1 Jan 2027Happy New Year — Rest Day
  • Hatsumode — optional first shrine visit of the New Year. A genuinely moving Japanese tradition.
  • Rest day. The mountain will still be there. Your legs need this.
  • Mimizuku Onsen — soak, recover, reflect. Japan's public hot spring culture is a gift.
  • Shared dinner at Monkey Rider — the lodge comes alive on New Year's evening
Day 17Fri 2 JanFinal Shred
  • Last full day on the mountain — make every run count
  • Return to Happo-One for a farewell lap of the best terrain in the valley
  • Return rental equipment to Central Snowsports by close of play
  • One final Echoland dinner and early night — big travel day tomorrow
Day 18Sat 3 JanHomeward Bound
  • Check out of Monkey Rider. What a week.
  • Coach from Hakuba to Narita Airport (~5.5 hours)
  • Last conveyor-belt sushi at Narita Terminal 3 — go hard, it'll be months before you get more
